Jam vs. Low traffic
Jam traffic breaches 6.4× more often than Low traffic (50.6% vs 7.9%).
Breach Rate by Drop Distance
Distance shows a threshold effect, not a slope
Breach rate steps up sharply past ~10km, then flattens (45.5% → 45.0%).
Breach Rate by Orders Batched Per Trip
A dispatch decision the business controls directly
Batching 2 or more orders is a near-guaranteed failure — 99.9% at 2 batched, 100.0% at 3. This is a switch, not a gradual trade-off.
A rider carrying two orders has to complete the first delivery before starting the second — the promised 30-minute window was set assuming one order per trip. Batching was likely introduced to improve rider efficiency (fewer empty return trips), but under the current SLA it trades efficiency directly for reliability.
Breach Rate by Vehicle Condition
0 = Poor, 3 = Excellent — condition 0 covers roughly a third of all orders
Conditions 1 and 2 are statistically identical (23.9% vs 24.0%). Only condition 0 is broken, at 41.7% — the single largest controllable lever in the dataset.
Trend Analysis

Time-of-Day and Daily Patterns

A stable ~30% average hides real structure underneath
Daily Breach Rate Over 8 Weeks
Toggle to see the raw daily swing vs. the smoothed 7-day average
Daily rate (raw) 7-day moving average
The raw daily rate alternates sharply between a ~15–19% "low" band and a ~43–48% "high" band, almost every single day, with no correlation to weekday vs. weekend. The 7-day average confirms the true rate is a stable 28–34%, matching the 29.8% headline — the problem is not worsening over the window.
Breach Rate by Hour of Day
No orders were placed in hours 1–7 (overnight)
Under 15% (Good) 15–45% (Moderate) 45%+ (Critical)
Mornings (08:00–10:00) run a clean 1.6–2.8% breach rate. The evening window 19:00–21:00 is worst at ~50%. Evening-peak and late-night hours carry near-identical rider load, ruling out simple understaffing as the cause of the evening spike.

The Recommendation

Which Fix Actually Pays — Costed and Ranked

Each programme modelled as a counterfactual: how many of the 13,597 real late orders would it have rescued?
% of ALL Breaches Removed, by Programme
The intuitive fix ranks third. "Coach the slow riders" — the answer most ops teams reach for first — recovers only 3.7% of all breaches. Fleet maintenance and a batching cap each outperform it, and together address roughly 4× the impact for far less organisational effort.
① Fleet Maintenance
Bring condition-0 vehicles up to condition 1. Recovers 15.0% of all breaches — 32.6% of its own affected orders.
② Cap Batching at 1
Never assign 2+ orders per trip. Recovers 13.2% of all breaches — the most surgical fix (76.8% of its own scope).
③ Coach Below-Median
Lift bottom riders to their city's median. Recovers only 3.7% of all breaches — intuitive, but the weakest of the four.
